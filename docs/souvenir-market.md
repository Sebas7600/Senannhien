# Phố lưu niệm An Nhiên

Eight permanent stalls on the two plots inside the tam quan: four at x=10.6 and four at x=-10.6; z=36.7, 43.5, 50.3, 57.1. The northern additions are fruit on the west and incense on the east. All face the centre avenue. Approaches, Rapier proxies, navigation obstacles and minimap markers use `souvenirData.ts`.

Design references: [Nakamise at Sensō-ji, JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1696/) and [Nakamise at Zenkō-ji, Nagano Tourism](https://www.go-nagano.net/en/trip-idea/id11580). Timber counters, compact repeating storefronts, hanging signs, split noren valances and paper lanterns are adapted to the Vietnamese temple setting. All new artwork and geometry are original procedural assets; no reference-site images are shipped.

Click a stall to walk to its front; press E or the nearby prompt to browse. Six categories: prayer beads, Buddha figures, Buddha pendants, wrist beads, sutra books and necklaces. Prices (6–24 in-game merit) live in the catalogue. Purchases are atomic, proximity-checked, require enough merit and grant one collectible per category. Repeated purchases never charge again. Manage owned items from Túi vải → Trang bị. Neck, wrist and two-hand slots are functional and saved in this browser; there is no real payment.

Produce baskets cost 4 merit each; incense packs cost 6 merit and add five sticks to the existing incense inventory. Supplies are repeatable purchases and do not count toward the six collectibles. Fruit can be used from Túi vải for eight energy, capped at 100; at full energy no fruit is consumed. Version 5 save migration supplies a default fruit count while retaining old inventory, merit and collectibles. Eight sign cells and two book-cover cells fit the same 1024² atlas.

The optional 360° inspector reuses the same procedural product builders and materials as the stalls. Its module and extra canvas load only after clicking the 3D button. It renders on demand, without automatic spinning, physics or shadows; drag or use the keyboard-accessible rotation buttons. Closing the panel releases the inspector canvas. Each cached inspection model is under 7k triangles including its smoother beads.

Rendering: two static plot batches, 16 primary draw calls excluding shadows, 477 instanced beads, 81,608 triangles at high quality. The same small canvas textures and one 1024² atlas supply wood grain, linen, signs and book covers. Low quality reduces bead geometry; small merchandise culls by distance at 4 Hz. Bounds are computed after batching. Night lighting reuses the existing two nearest spotlights instead of allocating eight new ones.

Countertop pendants and necklaces now lie face-up on linen-lined trays. Each ornament is positioned using its actual mesh bounds, with 0.5 mm contact clearance, and its relief is shallower for a convincing flat cameo. Cords lie on the tray; supported hanging samples remain on the rear display. The optional inspection models remain upright for convenient viewing.

All eight stalls have an ATT900-style dual-display terminal on the customer-left corner, following the existing dining reception terminal. `COUNTER_LAYOUT` reserves that area and shifts countertop merchandise into three compact bays to its right. The terminals add three draw calls: eight instanced shells plus two material groups on one merged screen mesh, 6,752 triangles in total. Customer-facing screens reuse the exact pink/navy check-in image from the dining reception; staff-facing screens reuse its colourful dashboard. Both original JPEGs are loaded in the same order and from the same URLs as ReceptionDesk, allowing drei to share the existing texture cache. The former generated atlas is removed; no additional screen artwork, extra light, animation loop or live DOM surface is required. Both screen faces and the body open the existing proximity-checked stall interface. Computed instance bounds keep the machines visible when turning the camera. Regression tests cover contact heights, merchandise clearance, face orientation, full-image UVs, material grouping and the screen-to-stall click mapping.

Camera sweeps use the actual back wall, counter and two roof slopes, leaving the open serving space unblocked. Real Rapier tests cover stalls on both sides of the avenue. Player navigation still uses eight compact static stall footprints.

Each stall now has a named chibi shopkeeper. `merchantData.ts` supplies position, gender, clothing, apron colours and a greeting. The shop and nearby prompt identify its seller. The character meshes are derived from the existing player GLB: the standing counter pose is baked offline, with the head and female hair separated at the head pivot. Eight bodies, heads and hair share three instanced draw calls, per-instance colour attributes, and no live skeletons or mixers. The existing stall footprint covers the shopkeeper, so no extra character physics is necessary.

The shared compressed asset is 155,392 bytes at high quality or 94,052 bytes at low quality, fetched once for all eight people. Full-set rendered triangle counts are approximately 90k / 48k. Revision 2 closes both stall sides with wood panels and solid gable ends. Matching side hitboxes stop the camera. A dedicated vendor stance retains the original neutral pelvis, legs and flat ankle orientation; it no longer uses the forward-reaching serving stance. Both arms start at unequal, relaxed heights. Arm weights are baked into the existing UV channel, and a tiny vertex-shader motion adds staggered greetings, left-hand adjustment, upper-body breathing and weight shift without translating the feet. The head follows the same upper-body offset. Motion stops outside 18 metres or with reduced motion; the eight sellers still use three draw calls. A revision query on the GLB URL avoids stale cached poses.

Head tracking is limited to ±0.42 radians and a very small nod; it stops outside 18 metres and respects reduced motion. Matrix updates run at 24 Hz / 15 Hz, with distance visibility sampled at 4 Hz and conservative padded bounds. Rebuild assets with `npm run prepare:merchants`; `vendor-budget.json` records the original model hash and actual asset budgets. `tests/merchants.test.ts` checks placement, motion limits, colours, both decoded LODs, finite geometry and preserved bounds.

Regression checks: `node --import tsx --test tests/souvenirs.test.ts` (also included in `npm test`). Covers access, path clearance, lake clearance, mesh budget, finite bounds, balance, duplicate purchases and save data.


The eight stall nameplates are compact timber shop signs, coordinated with the wood stalls and fabric noren. A matte walnut face carries quiet ivory serif lettering, a material subtitle and one small lotus seal in the corresponding stall cloth colour. `souvenirSignArt.ts` paints them once into the existing 1024² atlas; book-cover cells are retained. Each board is 2.50 × 0.56 m at local y=3.18, z=2.18, in front of the roof fascia. Thin wood backing and two braces remain merged into the existing plot batches. Removing the heavy metal edging saves 192 triangles over the earlier lacquer version, with no added draw calls or texture downloads. The atlas uses a rough lit material with a modest 0.18 emissive contribution for night readability; it does not require extra lights or animation updates.


Inventory version 6: all seven purchasable consumables are assigned to a physical stall in `shopData.ts`. Fruit, fish feed, flowers and seeds are sold by Cô Thu; incense, candles and floating lanterns by Chú Bình. `buySouvenir` validates the product-specific stall, balance and water-carrying state before charging; the legacy `buy` delegates to this same validation. Túi vải has Consumables and Equipment tabs and no purchase buttons. It shows stock, use actions and navigation to the correct seller. Harvested vegetables remain garden-only. Existing supplies, collection and merit are preserved.

Six owned equipment types share three exclusive slots: necklace/pendant, bracelet, and Buddha/mala/book in both hands. They use cached vertex-coloured geometry, one draw per active slot, no downloaded textures or additional skeletons. Bone portals follow torso/wrist; a small IK overlay holds palms close to the carried object while preserving the walking leg animation. Hand props stow automatically during seated activities, rituals, sweeping and water carrying. Equipment is saved and synchronised to peers through the existing position update, with slot IDs sanitised by the server.

Fruit may restore eight energy or be offered at one of four supported altars. A local saved offering consumes one basket, adds two merit, displays a small shared-geometry plate and occupies that altar's fruit spot for eight minutes. These fruit plates are local to the player's saved journey; unlike the existing incense/flower/candle rituals they are not shared server offerings. Plate instances are capped at four and updated with the existing session clock. Migration filters expired/invalid offerings and equipment not owned by the player.
